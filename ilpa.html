Timeline — what changed and why it matters

2019

ILPA Principles 3.0 + GP-led Secondary Fund Restructurings guidance

  • First formal LP playbook for GP-leds: engage the LPAC early on rationale, advisor selection, and conflicts.
  • Introduced the expectations that became market: competitive price discovery, a status-quo roll option, GP rollover of crystallized carry, and full disclosure of fees paid to advisors.
  • Principles 3.0 reset LP expectations on fees, carry (whole-fund waterfalls preferred), key person, and LPAC governance.

Why it matters: Set the vocabulary — status quo option, LPAC conflict clearance — every later document builds on.

May 2023

Continuation Fund Guidance

  • Election windows of at least 30 calendar days (20 business days); rolling LPs should be “no worse off” — a genuine status-quo option on existing economics.
  • Disclose everything that moves value: GP fee/carry/rollover, advisor compensation, staple terms, alternatives considered, transaction cost allocation.
  • Competitive process run by an independent advisor; fairness or valuation opinion where appropriate; LPAC engaged before launch, not at signing.

Why it matters: The operating standard for every CV run in 2023–26; most sponsors follow it “to the extent applicable” (Kirkland).

Aug 2023 → Jun 2024

SEC Private Fund Adviser Rules — adopted, then vacated

  • Would have required a fairness or valuation opinion for every adviser-led secondary plus disclosure of material relationships with the opinion provider.
  • The Fifth Circuit vacated the rules in June 2024; the Advisers Act antifraud and fiduciary framework, LPA terms, and ILPA norms govern today.
  • Fairness opinions remain common practice on larger deals but are no longer a federal mandate (recent data put them at ~11% of volume).

Why it matters: The classic trap: candidates who say “the SEC requires a fairness opinion” reveal stale knowledge.

Jul 2024

NAV-Based Facilities Guidance

  • NAV loans should be disclosed to LPs and, where used to fund distributions, approved by the LPAC; LPAs should address them explicitly.
  • Ongoing reporting of facility size, cost, covenants, and use of proceeds.
  • Distinguishes offense (follow-ons) from defense (levered DPI) and asks GPs to justify the latter.

Why it matters: Secondary buyers now request facility terms in every LP-led data room.

Jan 2025 → Q1 2026

Reporting Template update + Performance Template

  • Standardized fee, expense, and carry reporting; a performance template with gross and net returns, including with-and-without subscription-line IRRs.
  • Recommended adoption from Q1 2026 reporting.
  • For secondaries buyers: cleaner inputs for rebuilding NAV and pricing the IRR flattering from fund-level leverage.

Why it matters: Better data quality tightens pricing — diligence friction is basis points.

Jan 27, 2026

Continuation Fund Disclosure Template

  • A standard template covering asset information, process, CV return profiles, and CV terms — the pack LPs use to start the roll/sell decision.
  • Designed to compress the information asymmetry in fast processes and to make legal review of commercial terms comparable across deals.

Why it matters: Expect advisors to deliver it with every election package; its absence becomes a question.

Jun 2026 (draft)

Updated Continuation Vehicle Guidance — comment period closed Aug 5; finalization pending

  • Step-change from principles to operational governance: the existing fund’s LPAC becomes an active gatekeeper for CV transactions.
  • GPs must demonstrate the CV serves existing LPs’ best interests despite the conflict — and show why it beats alternatives: sale, extension, NAV facility, preferred equity, strip, tender, cross-fund transfer, mid-life co-invest.
  • Distribute the disclosure template; adopt a CV policy available to LPs; disclosure parity between buyers and LPs; protect rolling LPs from economic disadvantage (including discussion of a “remain in place” option); extend side-letter protections; documented, competitive price discovery.
  • Industry pushback (Kirkland): preserve flexibility for targeted or abbreviated processes; remain-in-place options require non-pro-rata ownership the LPA may not permit and create cross-vehicle conflicts; guidance is calibrated to single-asset U.S. deals. CFA Institute formally supported the draft.

Why it matters: If adopted, process integrity becomes the price protection: the LPAC’s questions are the market’s questions.

The structural point

Every row above is machinery for one conflict: the GP owes the selling fund the highest price and controls the buying vehicle that wants the lowest workable price. ILPA’s evolution — principles (2019), standards (2023), templates (2026), and now an LPAC gatekeeper model (2026 draft) — is the market admitting that process, not price, is the only durable protection. Note the draft is still a draft as of early October 2026; cite it as proposed, and check ILPA for the final text.
