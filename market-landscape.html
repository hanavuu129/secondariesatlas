The headline: H1 2026 was a structural turning point

The first half of 2026 set a volume record — but the bigger story is the mix. For the first time ever, GP-led volume overtook LP-led volume. The market's center of gravity has shifted from LPs selling fund stakes to sponsors engineering their own liquidity. Per Evercore's H1 2026 Secondary Market Review, GP-led deals grew 35% while LP-leds grew just 4% — a handover most practitioners view as structural, not cyclical, given that traditional exit channels (IPO, M&A) have lagged private markets AUM growth for years.

Say it in 30 seconds

"The market did roughly $121 billion in the first half — a record — but the real story is that GP-leds overtook LP-leds for the first time, at about 54% of volume. Single-asset CVs alone were $34 billion, up almost 90%. Meanwhile dry powder is down to about $194 billion, roughly one turn of annual volume — so it's arguably a demand-constrained market now, which keeps buyers selective and pricing disciplined. That capital scarcity is exactly why LPs keep committing to the space."

The pattern to internalize: discounts compress where information and alignment are strongest. GP-led deals price nearest to (or above) NAV because buyers get direct sponsor access and diligence; tail-end LP portfolios price widest because information is stale and duration uncertain. Pricing is the market's measure of underwriting confidence, not generosity.

Seven themes to speak to

  1. The structural handover. GP-leds are no longer the "fourth exit path" — in H1 2026 they were arguably the first. Sponsors are using CVs proactively to hold trophy assets and manufacture DPI, not as a last resort.
  2. Single-asset dominance. SACVs are ~53% of GP-led volume; per Coller, ~29% of CV deals now exceed $1B. Concentration means buyers underwrite these like direct deals — which is reshaping who gets hired onto secondaries teams (direct-deal skills increasingly valued).
  3. Capital scarcity flipping the market. Volume growth has outstripped fundraising since 2023. With ~1.0x overhang, buyers can be selective — but it also means H2 fundraising (Evercore estimates $200B+ needed over the next 12 months; Campbell Lutyens projects $130–145B) is the number to watch.
  4. Credit secondaries broke out. H1 2026 volume alone beat all of 2025. Drivers: direct-lending AUM maturing, BDC redemption pressure, new dedicated funds and evergreen entrants. If a firm has a credit secondaries effort, expect a question on it.
  5. Software caution. Technology's share of single-asset CV volume fell (roughly 27% to 20% per Lazard) on AI-disruption fears, compressed public comps, and uneven performance — a majority of surveyed investors report added scrutiny on software. Have a view on underwriting software durability in an AI world.
  6. Structuring innovation. Purchase-price deferrals appeared in ~25% of 2025 continuation funds (up sharply, per William Blair), bridging bid-ask gaps; ~25% of CV deals included premium carry tiers (per Lazard); fairness opinions fell to ~11% of volume — buyers relying on their own underwriting over third-party validation.
  7. The democratization wave. Evergreen and 40-Act vehicles are a fast-growing share of the buyer base, and retail access keeps expanding — changing fundraising, deal sizing, and liquidity management across the industry.
