Accounting & Valuation Core

The three statements, linked

  • Net income flows from the income statement to the top of the cash flow statement and into retained earnings on the balance sheet.
  • CFS adjusts NI for non-cash items (D&A, stock comp), working capital changes, capex, and financing; ending cash goes to the balance sheet.
  • Balance sheet must balance: assets = liabilities + equity, tied by cash and retained earnings.
The classic: walk me through $10 of depreciation (40% tax rate)

IS: pre-tax income −$10, taxes −$4, net income −$6.
CFS: start NI −$6, add back +$10 D&A (non-cash) → cash +$4.
BS: assets: cash +$4, PP&E −$10 → −$6 total. Equity: retained earnings −$6. Balanced. The punchline: depreciation is non-cash but saves real cash through the tax shield.

Deferred revenue vs. accounts receivable

Deferred revenue: cash received before earning it — a liability (you owe the service). AR: revenue earned before cash arrives — an asset. Deferred revenue growth is a great sign in software (customers prepay); AR growth outpacing revenue is a red flag (channel stuffing, collection issues).

Why can EV/EBITDA be compared across capital structures but P/E can't?

EV includes all claims (debt + equity) and EBITDA is pre-interest, so both numerator and denominator are capital-structure-neutral. P/E mixes an equity-only numerator with an after-interest denominator, so leverage distorts it.

Two companies, identical financials — one trades at 12x, one at 8x. Why?

Forward expectations: growth durability, margin trajectory, reinvestment needs, competitive moat, management quality, end-market cyclicality, liquidity/float, or the 8x business has hidden risks (customer concentration, litigation, regulation). Multiples are compressed DCFs — a multiple gap is a growth/risk gap.
