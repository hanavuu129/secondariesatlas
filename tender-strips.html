TRANSACTION TYPES

Tender Offers & Strip Sales

The lighter-touch GP-led structures: tenders move LP interests without touching assets; strip sales move a proportional slice of assets without picking winners. Smaller than CVs by volume, but every secondaries professional needs both cold.

Tender offers — how they work

  1. GP (usually via an advisor) invites secondary buyers to bid for the right to offer liquidity to its LP base.
  2. Winning buyer sets a price — % of NAV — open to all LPs for an election window (weeks).
  3. LPs individually choose: sell some, all, or nothing. Oversubscription triggers proration against the buyer’s cap.
  4. Interests transfer; the fund itself continues unchanged. Often paired with a staple — a primary commitment to the GP’s next fund.

Low uptake is common — tenders routinely see single-digit to ~20% election rates, because LPs who like the fund simply hold. That optionality is the structure’s core feature: nobody is forced to do anything.

Market context & reported examples

  • Venture is tender country: VC funds rarely fit CVs (concentration limits, board dynamics), so liquidity-seeking VC platforms have leaned on tenders — with mixed success, as several large reported attempts (including by high-profile crossover and venture firms) stumbled on the NAV credibility gap.
  • Strip sales as DPI machines: reported examples include large venture and growth managers selling ~$1B-scale strips of portfolio positions to secondary consortia — partial liquidity that keeps the GP’s story (and upside) intact.
  • Staples under scrutiny: as fundraising stays hard, staple ratios have crept into more processes; disclosure to tendering LPs is the governance frontier.

Interview questions — basic to advanced

BASICWhat's the difference between a tender offer and a continuation vehicle?

In a tender, LP interests change hands but the fund itself is untouched — no asset moves, no new vehicle, the GP just facilitates a buyer offering liquidity to any LP who wants it. In a CV, assets are sold out of the old fund into a new one with reset terms. Tenders are lighter-touch: no asset-level pricing fight, usually no fairness opinion, and full optionality for every LP.

BASICWhat is a strip sale?

The fund sells a proportional slice — say 20% of every asset (or of a defined subset) — into a new vehicle backed by secondary capital. LPs get partial liquidity while keeping most of their upside, and because the strip is pro-rata, there’s no cherry-picking fight over which assets go.

INTERMEDIATEWhy would a GP choose a tender over a CV?

When the goal is LP liquidity rather than asset duration: the GP doesn’t need more time or capital for any specific company, but its LP base wants an exit ramp — common for older funds, or GPs mid-fundraise wanting to show responsiveness. Tenders are faster, cheaper, and conflict-light because the GP isn’t on both sides of an asset sale. The trade: no fresh capital enters the assets, and the GP’s economics aren’t reset.

INTERMEDIATEWhat is a staple, and how does a buyer actually price it?

A stapled tender pairs the secondary purchase with a commitment to the GP’s new flagship fund (e.g., “for every $3 of secondary, $1 of primary”). The buyer prices the package: if the primary commitment is worth less to them than par — blind pool, fees, J-curve — they recover it by bidding lower on the secondary leg. Sellers effectively subsidize the GP’s fundraise, which is why ILPA pushes for disclosure of staple terms to the LPs being tendered.

ADVANCEDWhy have VC tender offers repeatedly struggled while buyout tenders clear?

The NAV credibility gap. Venture marks embed illiquid, model-based valuations of businesses whose outcomes are power-law distributed — buyers demand 25–40% discounts to underwrite that uncertainty, while VC LPs anchor on carrying value and often prefer to wait for the next markup. Bid-ask stays wide, uptake disappoints. Several high-profile venture tender attempts in recent years cleared only partially or repriced downward for exactly this reason.

ADVANCEDIn a strip sale, where do the conflicts hide if the strip is pro-rata?

Three places: (1) pricing — a single blended price across a strip of assets can overweight the strong names to justify the total; (2) scope — “pro-rata” across a chosen subset is still a choice: excluding the two best assets makes it a curated portfolio wearing pro-rata clothes; (3) economics — the new vehicle’s fees/carry accrue to the same GP, so the sale terms set their own future compensation. Buyers respond by pricing asset-by-asset internally even when quoting a single number.
