Co-Investments

Direct investment alongside a GP’s fund, typically fee-free — the fastest-growing corner of LP portfolios and the natural sibling of secondaries: same GP-relationship engine, single-asset instead of portfolio underwriting.

Mechanics

  • Why GPs offer it: to write bigger equity checks than the fund's concentration limits allow, to deepen LP relationships (a fundraising sweetener), and to reduce reliance on syndication.
  • Why LPs love it: fee drag on a 2/20 fund is enormous — co-invest at 0/0 can add 300–500bps of net return; plus deal selection and faster deployment.
  • Syndicated vs. co-underwriting: most co-invests are offered post-signing with 2–4 week windows (LP relies heavily on GP diligence). Co-underwriting means being in the deal pre-signing with real diligence access — harder, more valuable, what dedicated co-invest teams (HarbourVest, AlpInvest, Hamilton Lane, Lexington) build toward.
  • The adverse selection question: do GPs show LPs their worst deals? The empirical answer is mostly no — the bigger risk is concentration in big deals in hot sectors at peak vintage, since co-invest supply spikes exactly when deal sizes do.
  • What the buyer's job looks like: rapid re-underwriting of the GP's model (stress the base case, kill the synergies, check the debt package), reference the GP's history in the sector, negotiate governance (board observer, information rights, tag/drag), decide in days not months.

Where the market is

  • Structural growth: as deal sizes rebound (mega take-privates returned in 2025–26, including the largest LBO on record), sponsors increasingly rely on LP co-capital to fill equity checks — co-invest supply tracks deal size.
  • Institutionalization: dedicated co-invest funds at the big platforms (HarbourVest, AlpInvest, Hamilton Lane, Lexington, StepStone) have turned an LP perk into a standalone asset class with its own fundraising cycle.
  • Mid-life capital: structured/preferred co-investments into existing portfolio companies blur into GP-led secondaries — the same buyers increasingly do both, which is why recruiting for the two overlaps.

Interview questions — basic to advanced

BASICWhy do GPs give away co-investment for free?

It’s not charity — it’s currency. Co-invest lets the GP write a bigger equity check than fund concentration limits allow, keeps deals from being syndicated to rivals, and rewards LPs who commit to the next flagship. The “free” economics are the price of deal capacity and fundraising loyalty.

BASICWhy do LPs want it so badly?

Fee math: 2-and-20 drag costs several hundred basis points of net return; co-invest at 0/0 keeps all of it. Plus deal selection (choose your spots), faster deployment, and a closer look at how the GP actually operates — valuable diligence for the next fund commitment.

INTERMEDIATESyndicated co-invest vs. co-underwriting — what's the real difference for the investor?

Timing and information. Syndicated: post-signing, 2–4 week window, GP’s diligence pack — you’re underwriting the sponsor’s judgment as much as the asset. Co-underwriting: at the table pre-signing with primary diligence access, real influence on terms, sometimes a fee for the commitment risk — and a much bigger team requirement. Platforms graduate from the first to the second as they scale.

INTERMEDIATEWhat governance rights does a co-investor actually get?

Usually modest: board observer (not a seat), information rights, tag-along, and drag obligations; consent rights only on narrow items like affiliate transactions. You are structurally a passenger — which is why underwriting the driver (the GP’s track record in this exact sector and playbook) is most of the work.

ADVANCEDIs adverse selection in co-investment real? What does the evidence say?

The clean version — GPs dumping bad deals on LPs — is mostly not supported: offered deals perform roughly in line with fund deals. The subtle version is real: co-invest supply spikes when deals are biggest, sectors are hottest, and vintages are toppiest, because that’s when GPs need the most co-capital. So a passive co-invest program overweights exactly the moments to be cautious. The fix is discipline on pace and price, not suspicion of motive.

ADVANCEDHow would you evaluate a mid-life (structured) co-investment — fresh capital into an existing portfolio company?

It’s halfway to a single-asset secondary: known asset, incumbent sponsor, but new-money terms. Underwrite (1) use of proceeds — offense (M&A, growth) vs. defense (deleveraging, covenant relief) changes everything; (2) entry basis vs. the sponsor’s — you’re often buying at today’s mark, so the mark’s credibility is the deal; (3) structure — these frequently come as preferred with a floor return plus equity upside, so model the downside protection honestly; (4) alignment — is the sponsor writing new money alongside you, or just selling you dilution?
