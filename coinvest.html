MODULE 07

Co-Investments

A co-investment is an LP investing directly into a deal alongside a GP's fund — typically at reduced or zero fee and carry. It's the fastest-growing "cheap beta plus selection alpha" corner of private markets.

Mechanics

  • Why GPs offer it: to write bigger equity checks than the fund's concentration limits allow, to deepen LP relationships (a fundraising sweetener), and to reduce reliance on syndication.
  • Why LPs love it: fee drag on a 2/20 fund is enormous — co-invest at 0/0 can add 300–500bps of net return; plus deal selection and faster deployment.
  • Syndicated vs. co-underwriting: most co-invests are offered post-signing with 2–4 week windows (LP relies heavily on GP diligence). Co-underwriting means being in the deal pre-signing with real diligence access — harder, more valuable, what dedicated co-invest teams (HarbourVest, AlpInvest, Hamilton Lane, Lexington) build toward.
  • The adverse selection question: do GPs show LPs their worst deals? The empirical answer is mostly no — the bigger risk is concentration in big deals in hot sectors at peak vintage, since co-invest supply spikes exactly when deal sizes do.
  • What the buyer's job looks like: rapid re-underwriting of the GP's model (stress the base case, kill the synergies, check the debt package), reference the GP's history in the sector, negotiate governance (board observer, information rights, tag/drag), decide in days not months.
Compare-and-contrast question

"Secondaries vs. co-invest — how do the skill sets differ?" Secondaries is portfolio-level underwriting: many assets, GP behavior, pricing discipline, structuring. Co-invest is single-asset underwriting at deal speed: closer to direct PE judgment, but exercised through the lens of someone else's process. The best platforms run both because the information compounds — CV flow informs co-invest views on GPs and vice versa.
