MODULE 03

LBO Mechanics

The LBO is PE's core machine: buy a company using mostly debt, use the company's own cash flows to repay it, and sell later. Equity returns come from three levers.

Value creation = EBITDA growth × Multiple expansion × Deleveraging Exit Equity = (Exit EBITDA × Exit Multiple) − Net Debt at Exit MOIC = Exit Equity / Entry Equity

What makes a good LBO candidate

  • Stable, predictable cash flows (recurring revenue, low cyclicality) — debt service is non-negotiable.
  • Low capex intensity — more FCF available for paydown.
  • Defensible market position and pricing power.
  • Operational upside — margin expansion, pricing, procurement, add-on M&A.
  • Clean balance sheet and a motivated seller at a sensible entry multiple.

Paper LBO — the 5-minute drill

You will be asked to do this without a calculator. Practice until the rhythm is automatic:

StepExample
1. Entry$100M EBITDA × 10.0x = $1,000M EV. Fund with 5.0x debt ($500M) + $500M equity.
2. Grow EBITDA10% per year, 5 years: 100 → 110 → 121 → 133 → 146 → ~161.
3. Debt paydownFCF after interest, taxes, capex ≈ $50M/yr → repay $250M → exit net debt $250M.
4. Exit161 × 10.0x = $1,610M EV − $250M debt = $1,360M equity.
5. Returns1,360 / 500 = 2.7x MOIC ≈ ~22% IRR over 5 years.

IRR ↔ MOIC mental map (memorize)

MOIC3 years5 years7 years
2.0x~26%~15%~10%

Shortcuts: Rule of 72 — money doubles when IRR × years ≈ 72. A 2x in 5 years is therefore ~15% (72/5). A 3x in 5 years ≈ doubling in ~3.15 years ≈ mid-20s.

Full model architecture

  1. Transaction assumptions: entry multiple, fees (financing ~2% of debt, M&A ~1–2% of EV), management rollover.
  2. Sources & uses: uses = equity purchase price + refinanced debt + fees; sources = new debt tranches + sponsor equity + rollover.
  3. Operating model: revenue drivers → EBITDA → D&A → EBIT → cash taxes.
  4. Debt schedule: beginning balance → mandatory amortization → cash sweep of excess FCF (usually to the cheapest prepayable tranche) → ending balance → interest on average balance. This creates the model's classic circularity (interest → FCF → paydown → interest); handle with iteration or by toggling interest on beginning balance.
  5. Returns: exit EV bridge, waterfall to equity, IRR/MOIC sensitivity grid (entry vs. exit multiple, leverage, growth).
PIK technical, done right

"How does PIK interest affect returns?" PIK accrues to principal instead of paying cash. Cash FCF improves (more sweep to senior debt), but the PIK balance compounds — at exit, a $100M PIK note at 12% for 5 years has grown to ~$176M, all of which comes out of the equity holder's exit proceeds. PIK trades near-term liquidity for a compounding claim senior to you. It flatters interim credit metrics while quietly eroding equity MOIC.
